# PPP Pricing Policy Research

## Evidence and interpretation

The World Bank describes a price-level index as a cross-country price-level measure. This can inform a regional affordability policy, but it does **not** itself create a defensible sale price or discount percentage. For skipwait.me, India pricing should therefore be a deliberate, stable regional price—not a claim that a formula has precisely calculated a user’s personal ability to pay.

The U.S. FTC’s pricing guidance requires a former or comparison price to be genuine, bona fide, and not artificially set to manufacture a discount. A local India price may therefore show a struck-through **global price equivalent** only when that global price is actually offered in the rest-of-world route and the display clearly labels the comparison as a regional/global comparison rather than a time-limited sale.

## User-centered policy

1. Detect India privately from the existing locale/time-zone signal, but allow a quiet billing-country correction before checkout.
2. Present one local price first. Beneath it, show the global reference price struck through and a small, numeric savings label.
3. Label the price as **India price** or **regional price**—never as an expiring sale, voucher, or “limited offer.”
4. Revalidate the route at hosted checkout and do not grant the India price when the billing country is not India.
5. Keep the comparison simple: the reference price, current local price, and exact percentage only. The explanation remains optional rather than a required checkout decision.
