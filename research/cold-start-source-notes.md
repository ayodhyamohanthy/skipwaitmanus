# Cold-Start Source Notes

## Evidence used

### Focus on supply and dense liquidity rather than broad registration

**Source:** [Sharetribe, “The proven two-sided marketplace playbook”](https://www.sharetribe.com/how-to-build/two-sided-marketplace/), 12 August 2025.

Sharetribe recommends recruiting the harder-to-acquire side first, focusing on a tight niche or location, and measuring liquidity rather than raw user volume. It also describes a “single-player mode” as a way to give supply value before demand exists.

**Application to skipwait.me:** Referrers are the scarce and reputation-constrained side. The product must give them immediate individual value—work-email verification, a controlled company inbox, a safe opportunity-posting surface, and concise referral-response tools—before expecting them to bring demand. The launch unit should be a company-role corridor, not an empty global directory.

### Test with real marquee users and value propositions on both sides

**Source:** [Nguyen, “Solving chicken and egg dilemma in online platform startup: value proposition in focus,” Aalto University School of Business, 2017](https://urn.fi/URN:NBN:fi:aalto-201711137515).

The qualitative multiple-case study reports that the examined platform companies combined tactics and gave priority to testing their services with marquee users. It frames initial liquidity as a value-proposition problem for both sides, not merely an acquisition-volume problem.

**Application to skipwait.me:** Establish a small group of genuine company or alumni anchors before public expansion. Every Job Seeker who comes through a corridor must receive a truthful route—an eligible private request, a referrer-posted opening, or an explicit coverage-building action—never a simulated match.
