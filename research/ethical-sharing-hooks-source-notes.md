# Ethical Sharing Hooks — Source Notes

## Recipient-benefiting referrals preserve the sender’s reputation

Washington University in St. Louis summarizes field and laboratory research by Cryder, Gershon, and John showing that recipient-benefiting referral incentives can recruit more new customers than sender-benefiting incentives. The article explains that the sender can gain a reputational benefit from helping a friend while the recipient receives a direct benefit that lowers the action cost of joining.

**Source:** [Washington University in St. Louis, “Research shows how businesses can win more customers, influence your friends”](https://source.washu.edu/2020/07/research-shows-how-businesses-can-win-more-customers-influence-your-friends/), 13 July 2020.

**skipwait.me application:** Lead every message with what the recipient receives: three included private referral requests for a Job Seeker, or a controlled private company inbox for an employee. Do not lead with the sender’s reward, claims of popularity, or pressure to broadcast.

## Truthful positive feedback can reinforce sharing and trust

The study *Social Feedback Promotes Positive Social Sharing, Trust, and Closeness* reports that participants shared more with peers who provided greater positive feedback. It frames positive social feedback as reinforcing sharing and strengthening social bonds.

**Source:** [Brudner et al., *Emotion*, “Social Feedback Promotes Positive Social Sharing, Trust, and Closeness”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10169536/), 2022.

**skipwait.me application:** Acknowledge only real milestones—such as a matching employee verifying or a private request receiving a decision. Never fabricate likes, teammate activity, urgency, milestones, or social proof to force sharing.
